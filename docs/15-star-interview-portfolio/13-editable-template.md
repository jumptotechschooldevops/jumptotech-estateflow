# 13 · Editable Template

**Prerequisites:** at least one of the 12 examples read
**Next step:** none — use this to build your own portfolio, then go to [16-student-portfolios](../16-student-portfolios/README.md)

Copy this for every STAR story you write about your own real, verified work — from a
completed lab, a passed assessment, or an incident simulation you genuinely ran yourself.
**Never fill this in with invented metrics, fabricated business impact, or anything you
didn't actually do** — see [README](README.md)'s honesty rule.

```markdown
## <Area> — <short title>

> Labeled training/lab work. <or: state plainly if this describes real, verified work
> outside this lab — but never claim real production experience you don't actually have.>

### Interview question
<the question this story answers>

### Situation
<2-3 sentences of real context — what was true before you acted>

### Task
<what you specifically needed to accomplish, in your own words>

### Action
<what you actually did — be specific about commands/tools/decisions, not just "I fixed it">

### Result
<the real, verified outcome — link to your actual execution record or incident report as
evidence: see docs/06-engineering-records/README.md>

### Follow-up technical questions
<2-3 questions an interviewer might reasonably ask next, based on your own story>

### Strong sample answers
<your own answers to those follow-ups, in your own words>

### Common mistakes
<what would make this story weaker if told differently — be honest with yourself here>

### 60-second version
<a tight, complete, standalone version — not a truncated first half of the long version>

### 2-3 minute version
<the 60-second version, expanded with specific technical detail you'd only have time for in
a deeper conversation>
```

## Tips for writing your own

- Base every story on a real execution record or incident report you actually produced —
  don't write the STAR story first and work backward to invented "evidence."
- If a real story is less dramatic than the examples in this folder, that's fine — a smaller,
  completely true story is stronger than an impressive-sounding invented one.
- Practice the 60-second version out loud until it doesn't need notes — that's the version
  you'll actually use first in most interviews.
